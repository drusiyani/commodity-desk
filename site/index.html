          What drove Claude's decisions

          Claude's strategy lab

          Every Sunday, Claude invents new trading strategies in a small, safe rule language, and Argon tests them on years of price history it has never been fitted on. A strategy only trades live if it makes money in most of the test windows and is unlikely to be luck, even after allowing for every idea the lab has ever tried. The ones that pass share one £100k account. Below: every strategy so far, how it did in each test, and how the live ones are doing.

          About Argon and its data

          Argon is an experiment: Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, trades ten commodity futures (oil, gas, metals and crops) on a pretend £100,000 account, against several simple trading robots and an AI forecasting model, each with its own pretend £100,000. It runs by itself every hour on GitHub. No real money is involved, and nothing here is investment advice.

          Where the data comes from

          • Prices: Yahoo Finance, free and delayed by 10 to 30 minutes. Hourly bars for the last 60 days, daily bars for 5 years.
          • News: Google News headlines, two searches per market, every hour.
          • Related markets: the US dollar index, US 10-year yield, GBP/USD and the S&P 500, also from Yahoo.
          • Kronos: an open-source AI model that reads price charts and forecasts the next 24 hours. It runs on GitHub's computers.
          • Claude: Anthropic's API, every 4 hours on weekdays, plus once a week for the strategy lab.

          How each competitor works

          Claude
          Keeps 40% of its account in a core holding spread equally across all ten markets, and trades the rest itself. It reads each market's 5-year history, the news, an ICT chart read, Kronos's forecast, its own lab strategies and what the other bots are doing, then decides. Long only. Hard risk limits are enforced in code.
          Claude's strategies
          Rules Claude invents in the weekly lab. Only ones that pass strict tests on data they were never fitted on trade live, together, on one £100k account.
          ICT bot
          Trades a chart pattern called "liquidity sweep, structure shift, fair value gap", long and short, risking 1% per trade.
          Kronos bot
          Trades Kronos's forecasts when a move looks clearly likely and big enough to cover costs; closes after 24 hours.
          Trend bot
          Holds a market while its 20-hour average is above its 100-hour average.
          LIT bot
          Trades the S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, Dow and Russell 2000 index futures and gold on 15-minute candles. It marks where traders' stop orders cluster (yesterday's high and low, the Asia and London session ranges, equal highs and lows), waits for price to run those stops and snap back, and trades the snap-back in the London and New York morning windows, in micro contracts, risking 0.5% per trade.
          Buy and hold
          The yardstick: an equal slice of every market, bought at the start and never traded.

          Every competitor pays 0.05% in costs on every trade, and a stop that price jumps through fills at the worse price, as it would for real.

          The honest caveats

          • Paper money. No real orders are placed. Real trading would face slippage, margin calls and fees this doesn't fully capture.
          • Free, delayed prices. Yahoo's data can be late, gappy or occasionally wrong, and a whole market can be missing for an hour.
          • Futures rolls. Contracts expire, so positions are rolled to the next month a few days before, as real traders do. Older prices are adjusted so the switch doesn't look like a price move. Yahoo only keeps contracts that are still trading, so the size of older rolls is estimated from the jump in its continuous series (see the table below).
          • Small samples. A few weeks and a handful of trades prove almost nothing; luck dominates. Treat early results, good or bad, with suspicion.
          • Backtests aren't the future. They show how simple rules would have done on past prices, after costs. Kronos is never backtested, because it may have seen that history in its training.
          • AI can be wrong. Claude and Kronos make mistakes, and their reasoning can sound more confident than it should.

          What's private

          Claude's individual trades, its open positions, its decisions and the thinking behind them, its comments on the news, and every bot's trade list are private. They're stored encrypted (AES-256 with a key made from a password), both in the code repository and on this site, so they can't be read without the password; this page decrypts them in your browser, and the password never leaves your device. Everyone can see the returns, the race chart, the lab's results, the backtests and the downloads above.
